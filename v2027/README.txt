ELECTRONICS · YEARS 7, 8 AND 9
Fifteen lessons, twelve slides each.

==========================================================================
WHAT THIS FOLDER IS
==========================================================================

A complete Key Stage 3 electronics course, ready to teach, in three
formats of the same content:

  index.html                  the chooser. Start here.
  preview-Y7-1-....html       one lesson, click through it in a browser
  Y7-1-....pptx               the same lesson as a PowerPoint file
  preview-all.html            all 180 slides on one long page
  CURRICULUM.txt              the whole course on paper
  assets/                     the fonts and pictures the pages need

Published at https://luacslides.netlify.app/ — the same folder, served.
Nothing is transformed on the way out, so what is there is what is here.

Every file here is generated by `python -m v27.build` from
newyeardraft3/gslides/v27. Editing a file in this folder directly will be
overwritten by the next build; edit the content files instead.

==========================================================================
HOW TO USE IT
==========================================================================

In a browser:

  1. Open index.html and pick a year, then a lesson.
  2. Click the small Present button at the bottom right, or press
     Ctrl+Enter / Cmd+Enter.
  3. Right arrow or click: next slide. Left arrow: back. Esc: leave.
     When a slide has a hidden answer, the first click shows the answer
     and the second moves on.
  4. A slide always starts hidden. Going back to a slide re-hides its
     answer, so the same slide can be used with the next class.
  5. On a code slide, point at one of the three cards on the right and
     the line it explains lights up. Clicking does the same thing on a
     touch screen. Useful when the question is "which line?".
  6. The page fits the window, so it reads on a laptop, a tablet or a
     projector without anything being cut off.

In PowerPoint:

  Open the .pptx and press F5 or the slide show button. The answers are
  built as click-to-reveal animations, so one click shows the answer and
  the next click advances. Speaker notes are on every slide.

The whole folder can be dropped into a GitHub repository and served as
a static site: no server, no build step, no dependencies. index.html is
the entry point. It is published at https://luacslides.netlify.app/ .

==========================================================================
THE SHAPE OF EVERY LESSON
==========================================================================

Twelve slides, always in this order. The class stops spending attention
on where they are and spends it on the electronics.

  Cover        The inquiry, then the aim: what they will be able to do.
  Starter      Two facts the inquiry needs. Retrieval, aimed at today.
  Inquiry      The same question, one photograph, talk to a partner, then the answer.
  Predict      One piece of the inquiry. Everyone commits before the reveal.
  Idea         The sentence that answers the inquiry, and a photograph of it.
  Build        Four numbered steps and the wiring picture.
  Code         The program, decoded line by line.
  Test         What success looks like, as something you can see or measure.
  Debug        A broken version and the fault to find.
  Check        One misconception, three answers, one reveal.
  Stretch      Two tasks for the pupils who finish early.
  Exit ticket  Two questions, then the sentence. Click to reveal the finished sentence.

The same shape in every lesson is deliberate. By lesson 3 the class
knows that the predictable argument happens on slide 4 and the broken
version is on slide 9, and stops bracing for surprises.

==========================================================================
THE PICTURES
==========================================================================

Every lesson opens on a photograph of the thing the question is about:
a bike light nobody is touching, a lift that will not move, a crossing
with nobody choosing the lamps, a motor that a pin cannot drive.
The words come after the picture, and the picture is the question.

The idea slide is the same kind of photograph, not a labelled poster.
The sentence on the left is the answer. The picture on the right is
that answer happening, so a pupil at the back of the room can see it
without reading a schematic.

There are forty-five photographs, three to a lesson: the cover, the
inquiry, and the idea. They were generated as photographs, in one
warm palette — oak, sage, cream, deep teal, and gold only on whatever
is switched on. They live in assets/img/v27. Wiring screenshots from
Tinkercad are unchanged, and still sit on the build slide.

==========================================================================
THE COURSE IN ONE PARAGRAPH
==========================================================================

Year 7 makes something respond: an output, an input, a rule, a number
kept, a sequence of states. Year 8 makes it measure: a range, a
divider, PWM, calibration, and a device with two thresholds. Year 9
makes it move: a transistor, speed, position, feedback, and a message
another machine can read. Each year is five lessons and each lesson
stands up on its own, though they are written in order.

==========================================================================
WHAT YOU NEED
==========================================================================

One computer per pupil or pair, with Tinkercad Circuits open. Nothing
else is required: every build is simulated, and the wiring pictures show
the simulated board. If you have real Arduino Uno boards, the same
circuits work on the bench with the same pins; Year 9 needs a transistor
(2N2222 or similar), a diode (1N4007), a 1 kΩ resistor and a separate
5 V supply for the motor and the servo.
