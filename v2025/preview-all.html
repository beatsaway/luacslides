101/131

On, then reverse

void setup() {

pinMode(7, OUTPUT);

pinMode(8, OUTPUT);

pinMode(9, OUTPUT);

}

void loop() {

digitalWrite(7, HIGH);

digitalWrite(8, LOW);

analogWrite(9, 180);

delay(2000);

digitalWrite(7, LOW);

digitalWrite(8, HIGH);

delay(2000);

}

IN1 and IN2

These two pins choose the direction. They must not be the same if you want it to spin.

analogWrite(9, 180)

Enable pin. 180 out of 255 is on for most of the time, so the motor spins quickly.

The swap

After 2 seconds the two direction pins swap, so the motor reverses.

106/131

The signal is the angle

A pulse every 20 ms

About 1 ms means 0°. About 1.5 ms means 90°. About 2 ms means 180°.

The library

Servo hides those pulses. attach(pin) connects it. write(angle) sends the angle.

Power and ground as usual. The third wire is the signal.

115/131

Turn 100 steps

#include <Stepper.h>

Stepper motor(2048, 8, 9, 10, 11);

void setup() {

motor.setSpeed(10);

}

void loop() {

motor.step(100);

delay(1000);

motor.step(-100);

delay(1000);

}

2048

Steps in one full turn. The four numbers are the pins, in coil order.

setSpeed(10)

A speed in revolutions per minute. Too fast and the motor skips steps.

step(100) and step(-100)

100 steps one way, then 100 steps back. A negative number reverses the direction.

127/131

Two layers, and light

Two kinds of silicon

One layer has extra electrons. The other has gaps electrons can fill. Where they meet is the junction.

Light

A bright enough particle of light frees an electron. The junction's electric field sends that electron one way only.

A diode is the same junction used as a one-way gate. A solar cell uses it to separate charges.

It charges a 3.7 V battery from the cell or from USB, and gives a steady 5 V out to the project.

A protection chip stops the battery being charged too far, or emptied too far.
