A motor needs 400 mA. A pin can safely give 20 mA. What happens if you connect the motor straight to the pin?

A

It spins more slowly

B

The pin overheats and may be destroyed

C

Nothing: the board limits the current

THE ANSWER

click to reveal

THE ANSWER

The pin overheats and may be destroyed.

A pin is a logic output, not a power output. 400 mA is twenty times its limit. The output inside the chip heats up, and the damage may not show until a later lesson.

THE FAULT

click to reveal

THE FAULT

There is no common ground.

The base current has to return to the pin's ground. Join the emitter's ground to the Arduino GND. Without that shared point, the transistor never turns on.

CHECK · ONE QUESTION

Why is there a diode across the motor?

A

To stop the motor spinning backwards

B

To give the motor's collapsing magnetic field a safe path

C

To reduce the current

THE ANSWER

click to reveal

ANSWER — B

To give the collapsing magnetic field a safe path.

A motor is a coil. Switch it off and the field collapses, and the coil generates a voltage spike that can destroy the transistor. The diode sends that current round the motor instead.

If you chose another one, say why. That is the useful bit.

MEASURE THE BASE CURRENT

Put a multimeter, in current mode, in series with the 1 kΩ. It should read about 4 mA. Change to 10 kΩ and see whether the motor still starts.

TWO MOTORS

One transistor per motor, one pin each. One 5 V supply can run both. Why can the supply do it when the board's 5 V pin cannot?

4 mA switching 400 mA is a gain of about 100. That is what a transistor is for.
